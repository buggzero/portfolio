The Nature Gap 2.0 webinar series

I wrote and sent this campaign to the email list I built at Justice Outside. It turned a national research report into a three-part webinar series for funders, practitioners, and policymakers, and each email had one job: get the right people registered and in the room.

Results

What it produced

  • 246registrants for Part 1, which grew from 97 as the campaign ran
  • 120people attended Part 1 live
  • 51.4%open rate across the first six campaigns on the list I built
  • 3.8%click rate across those campaigns
  • 15,347emails delivered at 99.8%, Sept 13 to Oct 12, 2026

Open, click, and delivery rates come from Emma for Justice Outside's first six campaigns after launch, which included the Part 2 promotion shown below.

My role

I owned it end to end

Built the list

I brought Emma to Justice Outside, set up the list and its segments, and route event sign-ups into it with subject-line prompts, so every registration grows the list for the next send.

Wrote every piece

Subject lines, preheaders, the marketing emails, registration copy, and the social posts that pointed back to registration. I wrote Part 2 for Latine Heritage Month and drew on my own bilingual background.

Equipped our partners

Panelists and their organizations got a six-email sequence and ready-to-share copy so they could promote the series to their own audiences. Development got a customizable template for donors and prospects.

Measured and followed up

I segmented the registration exports by audience, from funders to grantees to researchers, so our Development team knew exactly who to follow up with after each event.

The sequence

How the sends were timed

  1. Pre-launch invitationAn exclusive early invite for donors, current funders, and warm prospects, sent through our Development team before the public promotion.
  2. Public announcementThe marketing email to the full list, with social posts on LinkedIn, Instagram, and Facebook pointing to the same registration page.
  3. Partner amplificationPanelists and partner organizations shared the event with their own networks using the copy I gave them.
  4. Follow-upRegistrants who couldn't attend live got the recording, and the registration data shaped the next send and the next event.

Part 2: what the data means for Latine communities

FromJustice Outside
SubjectWhat the nature gap means for Latine communities
PreviewSeptember 22, 9:00 to 10:30 a.m. Pacific. Save your spot.

Dear friends of Justice Outside,

Somebody taught you how to be outside. A grandmother with a garden, or a tío who knew which plants were good for what. For a lot of Latine families that knowledge has crossed generations and borders, and it never stopped.

Nature Gap 2.0 found something else. Of the communities of color living in this country's most nature-deprived places, 54 percent are Latine. The report puts a number on what our families have lived without needing the data.

This Latine Heritage Month, we are talking about both.

Nature Gap 2.0 Series, Part 2: What the data means for Latine communities
