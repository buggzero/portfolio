About me

Hi, I'm Bren. Let's make something people stop for.

I grew up in Mexico City and have written and designed in English and Spanish from the start. Colorado Springs is home now. I'm a full-on extrovert and a proud yapper, so I'll probably ask about your weekend before we ever talk headlines or fonts.

That's on purpose. The best work I've made started with a real conversation. Before I write a word or open InDesign, I want to know who you are, who you're trying to reach, and what you want them to do next. Then the speech, the report, or the event sounds like you.

For 13+ years I've led communications that way: editorial strategy and content calendars, executive speeches and op-eds, media relations, flagship reports, and events. I turn research and policy into stories that funders, journalists, and policymakers can use, and I coach my team to do the same. Come say hi. I promise to listen as much as I talk.

Justice Outside fills a unique role in the philanthropic sector as an intermediary led by People of Color. How I see it

Every piece has someone on the other end, and I picture them first. Maybe that's a funder weighing a grant, a reporter on deadline, or a parent reading a flyer in a clinic waiting room. My job is to make the important thing easy to find and worth their time. That takes editorial judgment, which mostly means knowing what the story is and what can wait.

I don't do it alone. I sit down with the researchers, policy staff, and program leads who know the subject cold, ask a lot of questions, and share rough drafts early. Hand me a round of edits and I'll say thank you and mean it. I lead my team the same way. I set clear priorities and standards, then give people room to use their own judgment.

Then I plan it as one story. A speech, a report, and an event should sound like one organization, so I keep the messaging, voice, and style guidance that hold them together. I design too, so the headline and the layout get decided together. If the parent in the waiting room reads the whole flyer, I did my job.
